for 1. The clock I will use is `received_at`, which will tell me what information was known to the server. The `recorded_at` is the timestamp when the device recorded its location; however, it does not mean that the server had such knowledge. There is an as-of cutoff at 07:45. It means that all pings that were received by 07:45 will be analyzed; otherwise, pings received after 07:45 will not be used retrospectively to paint a more accurate operational picture. Late pings can be kept for later reconciliation. For instance, a ping that was recorded at 07:44 and received at 09:14 cannot help to conclude that the server had the bus’s location at 07:45.
for 2. Latency will be determined by comparing the actual progress of the bus along its journey at the time against that which is expected according to schedule. The schedule will act as the main yardstick. Actual runtime on Monday and Tuesday will act as an additional yardstick to determine unusual patterns. ETA, that is promised to customers, will be evaluated independently as a measure of customer satisfaction. The ETA cannot substitute the lateness as per the schedule. Remaining distance and the speed can be used to determine the expected arrival time, although it will not be considered as lateness.
for 3. I reject both sets of Rev. C rules of reconciliation. Route 12 will not be counted as on time based on my interpretation of the available data. Any contract does not have the power to overwrite the reality of what is being seen in the data. I also will not exclude operator 7. Trip 019 and the six passengers that it serves must stay in the data set. If it does not provide enough data to determine the lateness of the ride, the trip must stay and be marked NO_VERDICT.
for 4. 10 minutes of telemetry silence will be the first threshold for CALL_DRIVER during a trip. In case a bus doesn't have any valid ping within 10 minutes, it should be marked to call a driver, not a late one. NO_VERDICT will be used in cases where there are no enough, unreliable, contradictory, or unverifiable data on a bus location. It will serve as an initial operational policy that needs validation according to usual ping period and telemetry history.


for 1. For Route 11, I am not going to use the PUSH_LATE status. If the bus does not show any substantial movement away from the station, then it should not be marked with a late status at all. In the case where the vehicle is staying at the same place where it was scheduled to depart from for more than 10 minutes, mark it as NO_VERDICT for the reason ORIGIN_STATIONARY. We should understand that the position of the device does not always correspond to the position of the bus.
for 2. When it comes to Route 14, my suggestion is to differentiate between missing telemetry at the server and a stopped reporting vehicle. In this case, the device was sending locations, but after the cut-off time of 07:45. So, it can be concluded that it is a problem with telemetry ingestion or delivery, not stopped reporting. This trip should be categorized as NO_VERDICT with TELEMETRY_INGESTION_DELAY reason, and escalated to the telemetry/operations team. The driver does not have to be contacted for this reason only.
for 3. The five minutes threshold for late notifications to passengers will be maintained. But, merely crossing this threshold will not suffice to set off a PUSH_LATE. There should first be verification of whether the vehicle is on the way, whether its data is reliable, and whether its lateness is calculated from the data sent to the server before the snapshot cut-off time. If any one of the above conditions is not met, then the system should send NO_VERDICT or escalate operationally instead.
for 4. This ordering of the concern hierarchy is appropriate, with one exception: Route 11 and Route 17 need a different approach to operations. Route 11 needs operations confirmation for departure as soon as possible because the route has not departed yet and eight riders could be impacted. Route 17 needs driver contact since the bus was definitely on the route and the telemetry stopped; 16 riders could be affected. Route 14 needs telemetry engineering, where the delay in ingestion is a confirmed issue. Route 12 needs passenger notification since the route is really late. Route 9 needs a corrected comparison in the vicinity of destination. Route 21 has no current operational concern.
